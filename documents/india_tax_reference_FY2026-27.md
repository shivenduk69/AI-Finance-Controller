# India Tax Reference Guide — FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28)
### TDS, TCS, ITR Forms & Income Tax Slabs

> **Disclaimer:** This is a general reference document for study/portfolio purposes, compiled from publicly available tax information current as of August 2026. Rates, thresholds, and section numbers change with each Union Budget and CBDT notification — always verify against the Income Tax Department portal (incometax.gov.in) before relying on this for actual filing or compliance decisions. This is not professional tax advice.

---

## 0. The Big Structural Change You Need to Know First

The **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961, effective **1 April 2026**. For FY 2026-27 onward, the scattered TDS/TCS sections you may have studied (194C, 194J, 194I, 206C, etc.) have been **consolidated and renumbered**:

| Old Act (1961) | New Act (2025) | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Section 192 (salary TDS) | **Section 392** | Salary |
| Section 193, 194, 194A–194T series (non-salary TDS) | **Section 393** | Most non-salary TDS, identified by numeric payment codes (1004–1067) |
| Section 195 (non-resident payments) | **Section 393(2)** | Payments to non-residents |
| Section 206C series (TCS) | **Section 394** | All TCS provisions |

**Important:** The old section numbers (194C, 194J, etc.) are retained here alongside the new ones because they're still how most practitioners, software, and study material refer to these provisions — the *rates and thresholds are substantively unchanged*, only the section architecture changed. Returns for income earned before 1 April 2026 are still governed by the old Act.

## 1. TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) — Key Sections & Rates

TDS is deducted by the **payer** at the time of payment/credit, whichever is earlier, and deposited with the government on the payee's behalf.

### 1.1 Most Common TDS Provisions (Resident Payees)

| Nature of Payment | Old Section | New Section (2025) | Rate | Threshold (annual, unless noted) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salary | 192 | 392 | Slab rate (average rate method) | Basic exemption limit |
| Interest on securities | 193 | 393(1) | 10% | ₹10,000 |
| Bank/post office interest (non-senior citizen) | 194A | 393(1) | 10% | ₹50,000 |
| Bank/post office interest (senior citizen, 60+) | 194A | 393(1) | 10% | ₹1,00,000 |
| Dividend | 194 | 393(1) | 10% | ₹10,000 |
| Contractor payment — individual/HUF | 194C | 393(1) | 1% | ₹30,000 (single) / ₹1,00,000 (aggregate) |
| Contractor payment — others (companies, firms) | 194C | 393(1) | 2% | Same as above |
| Commission/brokerage | 194H | 393(1) | 2% | ₹20,000 |
| Rent — plant & machinery | 194I | 393(1) | 2% | ₹2,40,000 |
| Rent — land/building/furniture | 194I | 393(1) | 10% | ₹2,40,000 |
| Rent by individual/HUF (non-audit, not covered by 194I) | 194IB | 393(1) | 5% | ₹50,000/month |
| Professional/technical fees | 194J | 393(1) | 10% (2% for certain technical services/call centres) | ₹50,000 |
| Purchase of immovable property | 194IA | 393(1) | 1% | ₹50,00,000 |
| Purchase of goods (buyer's turnover > ₹10 cr) | 194Q | 393(1) | 0.1% | ₹50,00,000 (per seller, per year) |
| E-commerce operator payments to participants | 194O | 393(1) | 0.1–1% (varies) | ₹5,00,000 (for individuals/HUF exemption) |
| Payment to partners (salary, remuneration, interest) | 194T *(new, from 1 Apr 2025)* | 393(1) | 10% | ₹20,000 |
| Virtual digital assets (crypto, NFTs) | 194S | 393(1) | 1% | ₹50,000 (specified persons) / ₹10,000 (others) |
| Cash withdrawal beyond limit | 194N | 393(1) | 2% (10% if ITR not filed for 3 years, over higher threshold) | ₹1 crore (₹20 lakh if no ITR filed) |
| Lottery, crossword, card game winnings | 194B | 393(3) | 30% | ₹10,000 |
| Online gaming winnings | 194BA | 393(3) | 30% | No threshold — every rupee taxed |
| Horse race winnings | 194BB | 393(3) | 30% | ₹10,000 |

### 1.2 No-PAN / Invalid PAN Rule
If the payee does not furnish a valid PAN, TDS must be deducted at the **higher of**: the applicable section rate, **20%**, or the rate in force (old Section 206AA rule, retained under the 2025 Act).

### 1.3 Compliance Cycle
| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Deposit deadline | 7th of the following month (30 April for March deductions) |
| Quarterly TDS return | Form 24Q (salary) / 26Q (other residents) / 27Q (non-residents) — due 31 Jul, 31 Oct, 31 Jan, 31 May |
| Certificate to deductee | Form 16 (salary, annual) / Form 16A (non-salary, quarterly) |
| Payee's record | Reflected in **Form 26AS** and **AIS (Annual Information Statement)** — used to claim TDS credit while filing ITR |
| Challan | Challan No. 281 |

### 1.4 Note: Section 206AB Removed
The earlier provision requiring higher TDS for non-filers of ITR in the prior two years (Section 206AB) has been **removed** effective FY 2026-27, simplifying compliance for deductors.

## 2. TCS (Tax Collected at Source) — Key Provisions

TCS is collected by the **seller/collector** from the buyer, over and above the sale price, at the time of sale/remittance.

### 2.1 Goods & Business Transactions

| Nature of Transaction | Old Section | New Section (2025) | Rate | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sale of scrap | 206C(1) | 394(1) | 1% | No minimum (per transaction) |
| Sale of timber/forest produce | 206C(1) | 394(1) | 2% | No minimum |
| Sale of tendu leaves | 206C(1) | 394(1) | 5% | No minimum |
| Sale of minerals (coal, lignite, iron ore) | 206C(1) | 394(1) | 1% | No minimum |
| Alcoholic liquor for human consumption | 206C(1) | 394(1) | 1% | No minimum |
| Motor vehicle sale | 206C(1F) | 394(1) | 1% | ₹10,00,000 (per transaction) |
| Luxury goods (watches, art, yachts, sunglasses, handbags, shoes, home theatres, race horses, etc.) | 206C(1F) | 394(1) | 1% | ₹10,00,000 |
| Parking lot / toll plaza / mining/quarrying lease | 206C(1C) | 394(1) | 2% | No minimum |

> **Note:** Section 206C(1H) — the general 0.1% TCS on sale of goods above ₹50 lakh by large sellers — was **repealed effective April 2025**, removing the earlier overlap with TDS under Section 194Q on the buyer's side.

### 2.2 Foreign Remittances (LRS — Liberalised Remittance Scheme)

| Purpose | Rate | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Education (financed by education loan) | Nil | — |
| Education (self-financed) / Medical treatment | 2% | On amount exceeding ₹10,00,000 in a financial year |
| Overseas tour packages | 2% | No threshold — applies from ₹1 |
| Any other purpose (investments abroad, gifts, etc.) | 20% | On amount exceeding ₹10,00,000 |

### 2.3 Higher TCS for No PAN/Aadhaar
If the buyer/remitter doesn't furnish a valid PAN or Aadhaar, TCS is collected at the **higher of**: twice the applicable rate, **5%**, or **20%**.

### 2.4 Compliance Cycle
| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Return | Form 27EQ, quarterly |
| Certificate to buyer | Form 27D |
| Credit claim | Buyer claims TCS as credit against tax liability, visible in Form 26AS, in the year it was collected |

## 3. ITR Forms — Which One Applies to You

The correct form depends on **source of income**, not profession or designation. Filing the wrong form gets the return marked "defective."

| Form | Who Files It |
|---|---|
| **ITR-1 (Sahaj)** | Resident individuals with salary/pension income, income from **up to two house properties**, other sources (interest, etc.), and **LTCG from listed equity/equity MF up to ₹1,25,000** under Section 112A. Total income up to **₹50 lakh**. Cannot be used if there's business income, more than two house properties, foreign assets/income, capital gains beyond the small allowance, or directorship in a company. |
| **ITR-2** | Individuals/HUFs with capital gains (beyond the ITR-1 allowance), income from more than two house properties, foreign assets or foreign income, or holding unlisted equity shares/directorship — but **no business or professional income**. |
| **ITR-3** | Individuals/HUFs with income from **business or profession** (not under presumptive taxation), including F&O trading and intraday trading (treated as business income, not capital gains). |
| **ITR-4 (Sugam)** | Resident individuals, HUFs, and **firms (other than LLPs)** opting for **presumptive taxation** under Sections 44AD (business), 44ADA (profession), or 44AE (goods carriages). Total income up to ₹50 lakh, up to two house properties allowed. |
| **ITR-5** | Firms, LLPs, AOPs, BOIs, and other entities not covered under ITR-6 or ITR-7. |
| **ITR-6** | Companies **other than** those claiming exemption under Section 11 (income from property held for charitable/religious purposes). Filed electronically with digital signature. |
| **ITR-7** | Persons/entities required to file under Sections 139(4A)–139(4D) — trusts, political parties, research institutions, colleges, and other entities claiming exemption. |

### 3.1 Key AY 2026-27 Changes Worth Knowing
- ITR-1 and ITR-4 eligibility was **expanded** to allow **two house properties** (previously limited to one).
- A new field for **"rent which cannot be realised"** was added to ITR-1 and ITR-4.
- Old capital gains rate-split fields (pre/post 23 July 2024) were **removed** since they're no longer relevant.
- Schedule AL (assets & liabilities) reporting threshold raised to total income **above ₹1 crore**.
- Section 80G donation claims now require **bank IFSC code and transaction reference number**.

### 3.2 Common Filing Traps
- Any equity/debt/gold/property sale beyond the small ITR-1 capital-gains allowance **invalidates ITR-1** — you must move to ITR-2 or ITR-3.
- **F&O and intraday trading are business income**, not capital gains — this requires ITR-3, even for someone who otherwise looks like a simple salaried filer.
- Commission income: if it's your main income source, it's usually **business income → ITR-3**, not "other sources" under ITR-1.

### 3.3 Due Dates (Standard, FY 2025-26 return filed in 2026)
| Category | Due Date |
|---|---|
| Individuals/HUF not requiring audit | 31 July |
| Businesses requiring tax audit | 31 October |
| Transfer pricing cases | 30 November |
| Belated/revised return | 31 December (or with late fee up to 31 March of AY) |

## 4. Income Tax Slabs — FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28)

Budget 2026 made **no changes** to slabs, rates, standard deduction, rebate, surcharge, or cess under either regime — FY 2026-27 rates are identical to FY 2025-26.

### 4.1 New Tax Regime (Default Regime — Section 115BAC)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

- **Standard deduction:** ₹75,000 (salaried individuals & pensioners) — no bills/proof required.
- **Section 87A rebate:** Brings tax liability to **zero for taxable income up to ₹12,00,000** (effectively ₹12.75 lakh for salaried taxpayers after standard deduction).
- **Deductions allowed:** Very limited — mainly employer's NPS contribution (Section 80CCD(2)). Most exemptions like HRA, LTA, and Chapter VI-A deductions (80C, 80D, etc.) are **not available**.

### 4.2 Old Tax Regime (Optional)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | Nil |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

- **Standard deduction:** ₹50,000
- **Rebate under 87A:** Taxable income up to ₹5,00,000 → zero tax
- **Deductions available:** Section 80C (₹1.5 lakh — PPF, ELSS, life insurance, etc.), 80D (health insurance), 80TTA/80TTB (savings interest), HRA, LTA, home loan interest, and other exemptions.

> The old regime is not the default anymore — taxpayers must **actively opt in** every year (for those with business income, the option to switch back is more restricted).

### 4.3 Surcharge (on tax amount, not income) — Individuals/HUF/AOP/BOI

| Total Income | Surcharge |
|---|---|
| ₹50 lakh – ₹1 crore | 10% |
| ₹1 crore – ₹2 crore | 15% |
| ₹2 crore – ₹5 crore | 25% |
| Above ₹5 crore | 37% (old regime) / **capped at 25%** (new regime — the 37% slab doesn't apply under the new regime) |

- **Marginal relief** applies so the surcharge never eats more than the actual increase in income that pushed you past the threshold.

### 4.4 Health & Education Cess
**4%** on (income tax + surcharge), applicable under both regimes.

### 4.5 Which Regime Should You Pick?
- **New regime** generally wins for taxpayers with **few deductions/exemptions to claim** (limited 80C investments, no home loan, no HRA) — simpler, wider slabs, and the ₹12 lakh nil-tax threshold via rebate.
- **Old regime** can still work better for taxpayers with **significant deductions** — large 80C investments, HRA claims in high-rent cities, home loan interest, or high medical insurance premiums.
- Since the new regime is default, opting for the old regime requires **explicitly selecting it** while filing (Form 10-IEA for those with business/professional income).

## 5. Quick Cross-Reference: TDS/TCS Credit → ITR Filing

This is the loop that ties all three sections together, relevant if you're building any reconciliation or compliance-checking tool:

1. Payer deducts TDS (Section 393) or seller collects TCS (Section 394) at the time of the transaction.
2. Amount is deposited with the government and reported in the payer/collector's quarterly return (24Q/26Q/27Q for TDS, 27EQ for TCS).
3. It reflects in the payee/buyer's **Form 26AS** and **AIS** — typically with a lag of a few weeks to a quarter after deduction/collection.
4. While filing the correct **ITR form** (Section 3), the taxpayer declares the underlying income and **claims credit** for the TDS/TCS already paid on their behalf, reducing the net tax payable or generating a refund.
5. A mismatch between income declared and TDS/TCS reflected in 26AS/AIS is one of the most common triggers for a tax notice — so this reconciliation step (declared income vs. certificates received vs. 26AS) is exactly the kind of check a finance-ops agent could automate.
